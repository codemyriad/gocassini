What Parakeet actually hears

Each participant’s track is decoded separately. A voice detector sees the full track, including silence. The updated Parakeet v3 path receives complete detected utterances with a little surrounding recorded audio, uses the model’s reference preprocessing, and adds no synthetic decoder silence.

Implementation status: the new path is enabled in normal builds in PR #304 and is undergoing a full-archive GPU replay. It has not been deployed to production. Historical comparisons below use source snapshot 61fb8f94.

Why the strategy changed: the previous 10-second windows and 500 ms decoder tail were workarounds. Arbitrary cuts reset decoder context; appended silence changes normalization across the whole utterance. The new path follows the model’s preprocessing and preserves real speech boundaries instead of tuning padding to individual phrases.

The normal path does not transcribe the mixed listening audio. It extracts each speaker stream into an in-memory float sample array and runs VAD on that array. Tracks can run sequentially or concurrently with a separate recognizer per worker. Each Parakeet window gets a fresh offline stream: decoder context does not carry from one window to the next.

How much audio goes in one call?

Silero uses a speech threshold of 0.18, a minimum speech duration of 100 ms, and 500 ms minimum silence to close a turn. Its configured maximum speech duration is 25 seconds. Completed spans are drained about every five seconds of input; that is a queue-management interval, not a five-second recognition chunk size.

The updated Parakeet v3 path does not subdivide ordinary VAD spans at ten seconds. It expands the span by 30 ms on each available side and decodes it whole. This margin follows Silero’s reference utility; it is not a measured universal optimum. A 55-second emergency split remains for unusually long detector output. Other model families retain their earlier window policy.

What happens around the edges?

  • Start of a detected turn: Cassini includes up to 30 ms of preceding recorded audio. Speech outside this expanded detector span can still be missed.
  • Internal decode seams: Ordinary turns no longer have ten-second decode seams. Adjacent expanded spans can overlap; matching words are merged using text and time alignment. Sentence-final punctuation differences no longer prevent matching identical words.
  • End of a decode: up to 30 ms of following recorded audio is retained, without appending a synthetic tail. Word times remain bounded by the source audio.
  • After recognition: an energy gate removes words unsupported by the original speaker audio. It allows 100 ms before and 200 ms after their timestamps for model timing jitter.

The energy gate requires a peak of at least −60 dBFS, RMS of at least −80 dBFS, and five milliseconds of samples at or above approximately −66 dBFS in that timing region. Speaker-attribution analysis is a separate later step: crosstalk is normally marked, with removal available as an explicit option.

A missing phrase can therefore originate at several places: VAD omitted real speech, a window lost useful surrounding context, the model failed to decode included speech, or later timing/energy filtering removed output. Changing silence padding alone does not establish which stage caused a particular omission. The parallel recording investigation tests those stages against the actual tracks.

The exceptional mixed-audio path

A guarded fallback can retry the merged audio when participant transcription is exceptionally sparse. It bypasses VAD and uses 15-second windows with 500 ms overlap. Parakeet v3 adds no synthetic tail here either; other models retain the earlier short-window padding rule. Its overlap policy can use a midpoint cut when adjacent hypotheses disagree completely. This is separate from normal per-participant transcription.

What upstream evidence tells us

There is no established universal optimum of 10 seconds, 500 ms overlap, or a 500 ms silent tail. NVIDIA’s model supports much longer offline inputs, and its buffered examples preserve substantial recorded audio on both sides of a chunk. Its stateful decoding algorithm differs from Cassini’s independent offline decodes. NVIDIA model card · NeMo buffered inference.

Sherpa’s offline Parakeet example decodes the supplied waveform directly, without a synthetic tail. Silero’s reference utility expands detected boundaries into the original waveform; that preserves real sound that zeros cannot replace. These examples justify testing both real context and synthetic padding, rather than assuming either setting is best. Sherpa offline example · Silero boundary utility.

What the recording experiments established

We ran over 1,200 recorded-clip comparisons across 19 clips, including independently selected meetings, short turns, bleed-only controls, and three earlier failure cases. Blind Gemini transcriptions supplied comparison references; these are model-derived hypotheses, not human ground truth.

  • Changes to padding can recover an opening while deleting a later sentence. The best-looking constants on one sample are not a general solution.
  • Sherpa normalizes each mel band over the whole input span. Adding PCM silence changes every speech frame’s normalized features, not just the ending. Feature path.
  • Modified beam search has a directly relevant upstream report of empty or hallucinated TDT output. Greedy decoding improved many cases but did not fix every omission. Upstream report.
  • On exact production PCM, NVIDIA’s published Transformers implementation retained the reported long opening in every tested crop/padding combination. Its short quiet phrase remained boundary-sensitive. Native experiments now confirm frontend parity but also expose separate beam-search omissions.
  • Numerical testing found a concrete frontend mismatch: reconstructing Sherpa’s settings differed from the reference features by RMS 0.29–0.41 on three exact crops. Matching the original model’s frequency range, window, framing, preemphasis, log guard, and variance convention reduced this to a few millionths. This is a principled repair target, but feature agreement alone does not validate a patched recognizer.

A same-model ONNX diagnostic held greedy search fixed and changed only feature extraction. With the existing 500 ms tail, a reported quiet phrase changed from empty output to recognized speech, though lexical errors remained. The longer missing opening survived both frontends under greedy search. These are separate frontend and search effects; the diagnostic does not yet validate a production replacement.

Native validation and a reference-based candidate

The rebuilt native recognizer uses the production ONNX Runtime version and CUDA provider. With experimental flags off, it reproduces baseline corpus scores. The compiled reference frontend matches saved reference features to within a few millionths across 12 comparisons; 32 short-input and silence checks passed.

A native beam-scoring inconsistency was reproduced synthetically: blank transitions could be scored for zero duration while advancing one frame. Correcting it did not change the final transcript tokens in 32 corpus comparisons. Final-candidate traces also show that the reported opening was already lost during search; changing final ranking could recover only a partial sentence. These are useful exclusions, not a claim that one small beam patch solves the incident.

The initial experimental candidate used reference preprocessing and greedy decoding, preserves whole VAD spans, removes synthetic decoder tails, and retains recorded context. The 30 ms central context condition follows Silero’s reference utility. We also tested 0 and 60 ms to check sensitivity rather than selecting a favorable value per clip.

Why vocabulary hints remain supported

A paired GPU comparison tested the corrected frontend and final boundaries with beam search, beam search without hints, and greedy search. The earlier comparison had not tested this full combination with beam search. On individual participant tracks, beam and greedy had 92 and 91 disagreements respectively over 834 reference tokens. Most of greedy’s aggregate advantage came from mixed audio decoded with VAD, which does not represent the normal participant-track path.

Beam search with hints retained both reported passages on complete tracks. Historical vocabulary clips demonstrated useful name and terminology corrections, while also reproducing false hinted words on bleed-only audio; that risk existed in the previous pipeline. Unhinted beam still lost a reported passage, so neither decoder is universally better. These findings do not justify removing vocabulary support. The final implementation keeps beam search and hints on normal participant tracks. The separately tested merged-audio recovery fallback retains greedy decoding, with explicit unapplied-hint provenance if it replaces the transcript. The validated INT8 bundle without the hint tokenizer also retains greedy; it cannot apply hints. Full-archive validation is being rerun with that decision.

A generic strategy, rather than new magic numbers

  1. Establish inference parity first. Validate waveform-to-features, encoder outputs, token durations, and decoding against the model’s reference implementation. Preserve vocabulary support only where the decoder is validated.
  2. Preserve utterances. Treat voice detection as a locator and scheduling aid. Keep recorded context around speech and prefer real pauses to arbitrary cuts. A maximum duration should bound memory and work, not stand in for an accuracy guarantee.
  3. Separate context from output ownership. For long speech, process real left/right encoder context but emit only the owned central region, carrying decoder state when the implementation supports it. Independent offline crops plus text concatenation do not reproduce this algorithm.
  4. Detect gaps and reconcile conservatively. Flag substantial speech without words and contradictory seams. Bound any retry, require agreement or reliable anchors, and never accept a candidate merely because it contains more words. Emitted-token confidence cannot prove that no speech was skipped.
  5. Test stability, not one favorable boundary. Vary boundary placement and use unseen meetings. Measure deletions, insertions, duplicates, false speech, timing, and cost. Keep references and selection data separate from held-out confirmation.
