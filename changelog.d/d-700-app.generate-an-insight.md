### Added
- Generate an insight from inside the app. Pick meetings in the browse list, open **Prepare context**, and the panel now offers a Generate card under Copy and Download: choose one of the templates this deployment ships and press Generate. The transcripts go to the AI endpoint you picked and the answer is written into your own Nextcloud files.
- Insight runs report themselves honestly rather than as a spinner. A run appears in the card the moment you press Generate and carries `queued`, then `running`, then a result or a failure — a local model over five meetings takes minutes, and the card says so instead of implying seconds. A failed run names the cause in words you can act on ("No AI endpoint is configured", "The endpoint rejected the request"), carries **Retry**, and — for an administrator — links straight to the AI providers panel that fixes it. Retry replays the request as you made it, to the endpoint you picked; it falls back to the deployment's configured endpoint only if you picked none, or the one you picked has since been removed — which is what makes adding an endpoint a fix rather than a suggestion.
- **Ask your own question.** One shipped template, "Ask your own question", takes a question you type and answers it from the meetings you picked: the answer in at most three sentences, the evidence with who said what, and what these meetings do not answer. The question box appears for that template and for no other — every other template asks its own question, and a question sent to one of those would be dropped without being asked, so it is refused rather than silently discarded. `cassini insight run` gained the matching `--question` flag.

### Changed
- A non-administrator sees no template picker: the template registry is admin-only, so their insight runs whichever template the administrator configured, and the card says so rather than offering a control that would be refused. On a deployment with no AI endpoint the card does not appear at all — the existing "No AI endpoint is available" notice takes its place — and in a standalone static export, which has no operator to ask, neither appears.
