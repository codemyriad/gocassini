### Fixed
- A sentence-final word no longer absorbs the pause that follows it. Parakeet stamps a trailing punctuation mark at the *next* acoustic onset, and the token-to-word conversion was letting that timestamp decide how far the word reached, stretching it across the silence — and across whoever spoke during it, which the viewer then drew as an overlap that never happened. Punctuation still travels in the word's text; it just no longer decides its extent. A word can also no longer inherit the previous word's end. On a real meeting this brings sentence-final words to parity with every other word (median 240 ms, p95 640 ms, longest 1.12 s, against 400/960/7201 ms before) and removes all but 0.4 s of the meeting's fabricated speech overlap.
