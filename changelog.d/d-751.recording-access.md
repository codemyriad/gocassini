### Removed
- **The Setup tab and its wizard are gone.** Cassini no longer opens on a question that blocks every recording until somebody answers it, and a link to `#surface=setup` now lands on Browse.

### Added
- **One dialog, once per install.** The first administrator to open a fresh Cassini is told who will see recordings and given one button that creates the `cassini` Nextcloud account they are kept in. Nextcloud asks them to confirm their password, and Cassini never sees it; the account's own password is generated, set and discarded. The dialog is recorded as seen by the install rather than by the browser. On Nextcloud releases that let an app write to user administration, Cassini creates the account when it is enabled and nobody is asked anything.
- **Operator › Settings › "Who can see recordings"**, with two audiences and one sentence each: **Everyone with a Nextcloud account** and **Meeting participants**. The one in force is marked, and the line beneath says how many recordings already exist and who can see them, including after a switch, when the recordings from before it stay visible to everyone. Where recordings are stored, the service account and the `occ` recipe are behind "Details for administrators". Where the `cassini` account does not exist yet, the section says so and offers to create it, whichever audience is in force.
- **The meeting list says who can see the recordings in it**, beside the meeting count: "Visible to anyone with a Nextcloud account" or "Visible to meeting participants". Administrators and everybody else see the same chip.
- **A switch shows its four steps while it runs**, with a count of the recordings copied, and says the page can be closed; one that was interrupted offers **Resume**. Choosing Meeting participants without Team folders or Everyone Group offers to install them, and says that Everyone Group adds a group called Everyone to the whole of Nextcloud first. The confirmation says what the switch means for new and for existing recordings, in a number, and the direction that widens the audience carries that number in its own button.

### Changed
- **Cassini works out who can see recordings when the app is enabled, instead of refusing to record until it is told.** A fresh install records as "everyone with a Nextcloud account", from the moment the `cassini` account exists. An install whose `Cassini` Team folder already holds recordings keeps "meeting participants", without asking and without moving anything, and recordings stranded in the other root are counted and offered a move. Cassini never widens an existing archive on its own, and "nobody has chosen yet" has stopped being a state an install can be in. A mode resolved this way reports `mode_source: "resolved_on_enable"`; a mode an older version recorded on its own, or an interrupted switch left behind, is kept and no longer needs confirming. Where Cassini cannot read the recordings it already has, it records no mode, says so on `GET /operator/status`, and looks again the next time the app is enabled; recording still goes ahead. The `CASSINI_STORAGE_MODE` deploy option is unchanged and still takes precedence.
- **A problem is one sentence now.** Where storage is genuinely broken, Cassini says it cannot save recordings right now, names the cause in words, and offers **Try again** and **Show details**; the step, the paths and the commands live inside that disclosure. Somebody who is not an administrator gets the same sentence and no buttons, and nobody is told that an administrator has to decide anything. The cause is written by the operator, one sentence per check, so `/status` and the app say the same thing about the same failure. A check that has not run yet, as after a container restart, is a neutral band rather than a warning, and the meeting list stays where it is.
- **The storage API reports the install as well as the archive.** `GET /operator/storage` carries `first_run`, answered once per install, and `migration` with the phase and counts while a switch runs. `GET /operator/setup` carries the mode, so the audience can be named for people who are not administrators. `recordings_access` on `/status` gains a `cause` sentence, and `/setup` carries the form of it that names no account, path or app.
- **The App Store description, the microsite and the documentation say who can see a recording before they say how it works**, with the default first and the opt-in after it, in the same words the settings section uses.
