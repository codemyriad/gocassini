### Fixed
- Playing a long meeting no longer pins a processor core. The viewer rebuilt the entire transcript's highlight sixty times a second and re-checked every word on screen in order to move one of them, so on the longest published meetings playback ran at about twenty frames a second, the page stuttered while scrolling, and the highlight visibly trailed the audio it was meant to track. The highlight is now worked out once per transcript and touched only when the playhead actually crosses a word, which took the worst meeting in the archive from using most of a core to using under a tenth of one, at a steady sixty frames a second. Clicking a word to seek costs about half what it did on the same meetings. The longer you listened the worse it got, too: playback accumulated duplicate timers until it was running forty-four clocks where one was needed, so a five-minute listen was heavier per second than a one-minute listen. Memory and timer count now stay flat — measured over a continuous five-minute listen on the largest meeting.

### Changed
- The word being spoken now lights up instantly instead of fading in. The fade lasted about as long as a short word, so the highlight was always part-way through changing and never quite agreed with what you were hearing; it now tracks the audio exactly. Hovering a word to seek also highlights it immediately.
