### Fixed
- The viewer now shows when two people spoke at once. Overlapping speech used to render as ordinary consecutive paragraphs with no signal at all, and because a turn is cut at every speaker change, a listener's "right" in the middle of someone's sentence split that sentence across two paragraphs with an unrelated turn wedged between them — so a continuous utterance read as two turns and an interruption. An interrupted turn is now drawn as one turn containing the interjection, each turn carries a badge naming who it overlapped and for how long, and playback highlights every turn that is sounding rather than only the latest one to start.
- Overlap is judged on the words that actually sound rather than on paragraph extents, and a word whose end ran past its own audio is pulled back before anything is drawn. Without that, meetings recorded before the timing fix would light up with overlap that never happened: across nine archived meetings their paragraphs intersect for 94.2 s while their words only sound together for 15.7 s.
