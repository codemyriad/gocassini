### Added
- A published meeting now records how its word ends were decided, as `provenance.wordTimings.endsBoundedByAudio` in the portable-meeting manifest. When the key is absent, a consumer may repair an over-long word by clipping it towards the meeting median; when present, the end was measured against audio and must be preserved.

### Fixed
- A word now ends where its speaker's own audio ends. Parakeet stamps a trailing punctuation mark at the *next* acoustic onset, and the token-to-word conversion took that mark's timestamp as the word's end — so a sentence-final word could run for seconds while its speaker was silent, and across whoever spoke during that pause, which the viewer then drew as an overlap that never happened. The mark still travels in the word's text; it no longer decides the word's extent. Instead the energy gate that already decides whether a word is real follows the speaker's own track forward from the last spoken token and stops at the last active 10 ms of it, bridging a silence of up to 200 ms as a stop closure inside the word. A word built entirely from punctuation carries no audio of its own and is never extended, and no word may pass the end the old rule gave it. Measured against unmodified `main` on a 36-minute three-participant meeting (5549 words, the production fp32 model), the longest word falls from 4.07 s to 1.44 s and cross-speaker overlap stamped over a silent track from 4.18 s to 1.69 s, against 1.37 s of audible speech given up across the whole meeting, two words of it losing 100 ms or more. Those are whole-release totals; the final refinement alone — dropping an 80 ms grace after the last active frame — accounts for 0.38 s of that speech and for no word losing 100 ms.
